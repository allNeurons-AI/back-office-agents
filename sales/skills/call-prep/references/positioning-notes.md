# Positioning notes

Read this whenever the Synthesize step in SKILL.md writes the Positioning section of a call brief. This file is the org's own point of view — the skill's logic stays generic, the actual pitch lives here.

An organization can replace this entire file with its own version — same filename, same purpose (state how to frame the offering against this account's real situation) — without touching SKILL.md at all.

## Default guidance (no org customization yet)

Without a customized version of this file, keep Positioning strictly evidence-led:

1. **Match what Gather actually found**, not a generic pitch. If the account's own stated priority (from an email, a call note, a public announcement) lines up with something specific being offered, name that connection directly. If nothing in Gather points to a clear angle, say the account's priorities aren't yet clear from history rather than defaulting to a generic value-prop paragraph.

2. **Only name a competitor if one was actually mentioned** in CRM notes, email, chat, or a call transcript. Don't speculate about who else the account might be evaluating.

3. **Keep it to two or three sentences.** This section supports the Objective card — it isn't the place for a full pitch deck's worth of talking points.

## How to customize this file

Replace the "Default guidance" section above with your organization's own point of view, for example:

- **Standard talking points** — the two or three things your org wants surfaced whenever they're relevant (a flagship capability, a recent launch, a proof point), and the situations that make each one relevant.
- **Known competitive landscape** — the competitors you come up against most often and the one or two sentence framing your org prefers when one is mentioned, so Positioning doesn't improvise a comparison on the spot.
- **What not to say** — anything that shouldn't appear in a pre-call brief even if it's technically true (unannounced roadmap items, pricing specifics that change deal to deal, anything legal wants reviewed before it's said out loud).

## Example: professional services / legal context

For a firm selling services rather than a product, positioning usually centers on expertise and precedent rather than features:

- Lead with relevant prior matters or experience in the account's specific situation (their industry, their type of transaction, their jurisdiction) rather than a generic capability list.
- If the account is comparing firms, frame around fit and track record for their specific matter type — not a head-to-head feature comparison, which reads oddly outside a product sale.
- Flag explicitly if this account or matter requires a conflicts check before the call proceeds any further — this belongs in Open items, not buried in Positioning.
